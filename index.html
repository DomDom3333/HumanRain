    How this is worked out

    One millimetre of rain is one litre per square metre. Standing still, you catch it on your horizontal profile — head, shoulders, upper arms — about 0.10 m² for an adult.

    Walking adds the bigger term. Rain hangs in the air at a concentration of rate ÷ 6 m/s (raindrop fall speed), and your front — roughly 0.65 m² — sweeps through it. That volume depends on distance covered, not time, which is why running gets you less wet overall but soaks your front faster.

    An umbrella removes the head term and cuts the front by about half; a hood covers roughly a third of the top and nothing else. A full outfit holds around 1.5 litres before it starts dripping — that's where "drenched" begins.

    Rough by design: real numbers depend on wind, drop size, and what you're wearing. It's for intuition, not for court.
