### New Features

- **The time outside reads forward from the hour you picked, instead of one figure stretched flat.** Picking a place opened on whichever hour the next shower happened to fall on, and the slider then applied that single rate for the whole time out — so three hours in a forecast that is dry until two and pouring at three came to exactly the same number as three quiet hours, which is the one case the question is asked in. Selection now starts where the question does — now, the first bar — and the slider reads ahead from there through the hourly series, adding each hour as it comes with part-hours counted pro rata. No approximation is introduced by this: both terms of the model are linear in rate × time, so a varying series collapses exactly to its own total. Tapping any hour, or any day, moves the start; the day strip opens on its own first hour rather than waiting to be tapped; and the bars the window covers are shaded on the chart, so the picture and the figure under it cannot disagree. Setting the rain by hand still works and now says so, standing in for the forecast until another hour is picked. Routes, which already walked the forecast on their own clock, now default to setting off now rather than an hour from now.
- **A week of forecast, a day at a time.** The horizon was three days and the hour strip ran off the end of it, so tomorrow's walk could be looked at and the day after could not. It is seven days now, cut into local days from the current hour on: the place view carries a row of day tabs each showing that day's total, and it opens on the day the next rain actually falls rather than on today by default. Route legs are grouped by day, the start-time picker spans the week, and a headline names the day when it is not today. A route that outruns the forecast says so and counts the overshoot as dry rather than quietly padding it.
- **Search finds landmarks and their routes, not just towns.** "Ötschergräben" returned nothing at all: the gazetteer behind the box only knows populated places, and the Overpass trail lookup ran an unbounded name regex over every route relation on the planet, which times out before it answers anything. Search now also asks Photon, which indexes every named object in OpenStreetMap. Anything that is a *line* there — a gorge, a ridge, a marked path, a route relation — is loaded as a walk with its real geometry, and everything else gets the forecast where it stands, so a search always lands somewhere: when geometry cannot be fetched, or turns out to be an outline rather than a walk, the location is shown with the reason given. Cycle routes and staircases are deliberately dropped from the walkable tags — this app puts a walking pace on whatever it loads, and offering those was a promise it could not keep.
- **A named path resolves to the route it belongs to.** A named way in OpenStreetMap is usually one segment of something far longer, so searching "Rennsteig" returned a 300 m stretch of track — short enough to be rejected outright as unwalkable, which is why picking the trail looked like it did nothing. A picked way is now resolved up to the waymarked route it is part of when the names agree, and the same search yields the whole 170 km.
- **The crosshair takes where you are standing**, loads the forecast there and reverse-geocodes it to a place name through Photon, falling back to coordinates where nothing is named — which on a phone is usually the question being asked. It appears wherever the geolocation API does rather than on a guess about the device, and the two failures are told apart: a refused permission is reported as the decision it is with no retry offered, a failed fix gets one.
- **The desktop has its own layout rather than a 560px column.** One column at every width left most of a monitor empty and squeezed the hour chart and the leg list into a strip. The answer now runs full width across the top, the picture on the left and the numbers on the right; under it an input rail carries everything you touch — search, GPX, route options, sliders — so what you adjust and the verdict it moves stay a glance apart, and the results column keeps the width for the day tabs, the hour chart and the leg list, which are the only parts of this page with a use for it. Giving the answer its own row is what lets the rail's rows be sized by the rail rather than by whatever the forecast happens to be showing beside it.

### Modelling

- **Rain only counts while it is falling.** Widening the time-outside slider exposed an error that a two-hour ceiling had kept quiet: the rate was applied for the whole time outside, so twelve hours out in a one-hour shower came out twelve times too wet. Only the overlap of the two counts now, and the slider says so when the rain runs out before you do.
- **Dry is its own answer, not the mildest degree of wet.** "Barely damp" printed over 0.00 litres reads as a forecast when it is the absence of one. Under a millilitre now reads Dry, in the muted grey rather than a wetness colour, and the head/front split draws empty instead of claiming that all of nothing came from the front.
- **The time-outside slider stops at seven hours.** It stopped at two, which rules out most of the walks this thing exists for; twelve, tried next, was longer than the walk anyone sets this for and spent half the slider's travel on hours that never get used. Seven covers the day on the hill and gives the minutes at the low end room. It is curved the same way the rainfall slider is — the difference between 5 and 15 minutes is worth resolving, the difference between 6 and 6¼ hours is not — with steps coarsening from one minute to five to a quarter hour as the numbers grow, and it reads back as "3h 30" once past ninety minutes.
- **The by-hand rainfall defaults to falling over one hour.** The pair of manual sliders is a rate, and reading it off took arithmetic: 1.3 mm over three hours is 0.43 mm/h, which is not a number any weather app puts on a screen. Over one hour the two sliders read as the millimetres per hour they are, which is what the hint underneath has always claimed. The stat placeholders in the markup are the pre-script first paint, so they move with the default: 0.05 L, 0.9 eggcups, 46 g.

### Performance

- **Overpass mirrors are hedged rather than queued.** They go down, rate-limit and stall independently, and a stalled mirror answers no sooner than a dead one — but they were tried strictly in turn with no client-side deadline, so one stalled mirror held the page for as long as it cared to and three in a row could sit on "Loading…" for minutes. A mirror now gets five seconds to itself before the next one joins the race; the first answer wins and the losers are aborted, so the volunteers are not left doing work nobody wants. The whole attempt is capped at 22 seconds, after which the forecast for the spot is shown with the walk offered as a retry rather than nothing at all. The mirror that answered is tried first next time. Forecast and elevation calls carry a 20-second deadline of their own.
- **The background trail sweep is called off when a result is picked**, instead of competing with the picked trail for the same server; a newer keystroke cancels it the same way. The Overpass name search is also fenced to a box around the geocoded hit, falling back to the worldwide query only when that box finds nothing.
- **The rain field empties instead of idling.** The drop count had a floor of six, so a dry forecast still drizzled; it now goes to zero, drains as drops land rather than vanishing mid-air, and stops the animation loop once the sky is empty.

### UI/UX

- The header no longer leads with the 1.3 mm slider default — the first thing on screen was a made-up number.
- Tapping an hour no longer jumps the page to the top when the answer is already on screen, which on the wide layout it usually is.
- A forecast with no rain in it says so and clears the sky; a route sets the field from the rate it actually meets.
- Weekday names in the rain headline keep their capital, and the slider's own bottom stop is singularised — it read "1 minutes".
- Hints and labels reworded around what the controls now do: the hour strip explains that the shaded bars are the ones being counted and that the count is the slider below it.

### Engineering

- **The stylesheet separates what a thing looks like from where it goes.** Everything above the LAYOUT banner is shape and colour and is shared; below it are two mutually exclusive blocks, the phone layout under 900px and the desktop layout at 900px and over. Neither is a patch over the other — the desktop places sections on a grid rather than overriding a stack of margins it inherited. The forecast output moves out of the finder section into a sibling, since it is the report rather than part of the search form, but sits in the same place in source order: element geometry at 390, 560 and 820px is identical to before, bar the finder box no longer enclosing the report.
- The search internals lost the moving parts they had grown: one elevation request instead of a batching chain, since thinning already caps a route at Open-Meteo's 100-coordinate limit and a second batch could never happen; a `json()` helper for the fetch-and-check-status dance every call repeated, and `fill()` for the stale-keystroke guard each source repeated; the two-failure counter, replaced by the same `Promise.all` that schedules the trail search reporting when both geocoders are unreachable; `note` off the module, the fall-back reason now passed to `showPlace` like every other piece of what it renders; and geometry handling split into `lineOf` and `length`, so the interesting part of `loadOsm` is what it decides rather than how it measures.
- The README was cut to what the thing is and how it works — the physics in two terms, the units ladder, the data sources and the AGPL obligation the footer link exists to discharge — and then kept in step with the week horizon, the seven-hour slider and reading forward from the picked hour.

### Bug Fixes

- Fixed picking a trail from the search results looking like it did nothing at all — two independent causes, the stalled-mirror wait and the 300 m fragment, both above.
- Fixed a route of more than 100 nodes silently coming back flat: elevations were requested in one call whatever the node count, past the API's limit, so a 120-node route had no ascent and therefore the wrong pace and the wrong time in the rain.
- Fixed a dry forecast still drizzling in the rain field, and the field animating on with nothing to draw.
- Fixed twelve hours out in a one-hour shower reading twelve times too wet.
- Fixed the time-outside slider reading "1 minutes" at its bottom stop, and weekday names losing their capital in the rain headline.
